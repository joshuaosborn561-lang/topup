# Voice and language

The reader is a founder, sales leader, or C-suite operator. Write for someone
short on time and long on experience. Do not explain what a cold email is. Do
not pad.

## Hard rules

1. **No em dashes.** Not one. Rewrite the sentence rather than swapping in a
   substitute character, because a document full of commas doing an em dash's
   job reads just as artificial. Use:
   - a full stop where the dash joined two independent clauses
   - a colon where it introduced a definition or a list
   - commas for a genuine parenthetical
   En dashes in numeric ranges (`Days 1–30`, `25–30%`, `$75,000–$110,000`) are a
   different character and stay.

2. **American spelling.** This is a Dallas company writing about the US market.
   Watch for `programme`, `dialling`, `modelled`, `labelling`, `deprioritised`,
   `organisation`, `prioritised`. Grep case-insensitively; `Programme` in a
   heading has slipped through a lowercase grep before.

3. **No filler paragraphs.** If a paragraph tells the reader something true but
   unactionable ("both are craft, and both matter"), cut it entirely. Every
   paragraph should carry a number, a constraint, a decision, or a commitment.

4. **Plain words.** `working` not `producing results`. `not started` not
   `unstarted and not deprioritised on the merits`. `about` not
   `approximately`. Delete `therefore`, `it is worth noting`, `in the interim`,
   `materially`.

5. **Short declaratives over compressed clever phrasing.** "Three months,
   $12,000 total, no renewal obligation." beats a 40-word sentence carrying the
   same three facts.

## What earns space

- Numbers, and the assumption behind each number
- Constraints and who owns them
- Definitions that make a commitment enforceable
- What the client has to supply
- The decision being asked for

## Modeling and claims

- **Show the chain, not just the outcome.** One row per step, with the
  assumption in its own column, so any single assumption can be challenged
  without discarding the model.
- **Never invent a conversion rate and present it as given.** If a step needs a
  rate Josh has not supplied, either ask, or state it in the document as an
  illustrative variable owned by the client. An invented multiplier inflated a
  lane 3× before it was caught.
- **Prefer published industry benchmarks over SalesGlider's own historicals**
  when the document has to survive scrutiny. Say so explicitly: "an accepted
  industry benchmark, not a number of ours."
- **Interested replies, never raw reply rate.** Raw reply rate counts
  out-of-offices and opt-outs as engagement. Say this in the document.
- **A guarantee and a forecast must not contradict each other on the page.**
  If the commitment is far below the model, say it is a deliberate floor. If it
  is close to the model, say it is set at what the lanes model rather than
  discounted. Never let the document argue against its own number.
- **Scope every commitment to lanes the agency actually executes.** Work the
  client's own team performs is additional and must be excluded in writing,
  otherwise Josh owes free work for the client's inaction.
- **Define the deliverable being guaranteed.** "Qualified meeting" needs a
  written definition or it becomes whatever the client says it is when counting.

## Section shape for a briefing

1. Recommendation (with a callout, and why now)
2. Situation
3. What already works for the client, decomposed
4. The one thing that changes, with evidence
5. Coverage or delivery model
6. Mapped against what they already run
7. Operating model and governance
8. Roadmap and planning basis
9. Commercial terms
10. Decision required

Sections 3 and 6 are the persuasive core: show that most of what the client
already trusts carries over unchanged, and that only one variable moves.

## Never

- Do not put a client's personal disclosures (health, family, legal) into a
  business document, even when they came up in the source call.
- Do not attribute an industry benchmark to SalesGlider, or a SalesGlider result
  to the industry.
- Do not leave document metadata crediting Claude. Set `creator` to Josh.
